Mindline

Personal cognitive tracking

A 10-minute check-in that compares you with yourself

Five short tasks measure memory, attention and speed. Repeat the check-in every month or so. Over time, Mindline shows whether your results are steady or drifting away from your own usual range.

What you'll do

  1. 1Learn a list of 10 words1 min
  2. 2Tap as soon as the panel turns green1 min
  3. 3Match symbols to numbers90 s
  4. 4Connect 1-A-2-B in order1–2 min
  5. 5Recall the words again1 min

Find a quiet spot, use the same device each time, and wear your glasses if you need them. With the voice assistant turned on, you start with a short conversation about how you're doing.

This is not a diagnosis. If you notice sudden confusion, trouble speaking, a drooping face, or weakness on one side, call 911 now. These can be signs of a stroke.

How it works

About Mindline

What it measures

The tasks are original versions of methods widely used in cognitive research: list learning with delayed recall (memory), simple reaction time (alertness), symbol–digit coding (processing speed), and alternating trail-making (attention switching). They are not the MoCA, MMSE or any licensed clinical test.

How comparisons work

Your first check-in is treated as practice, because most people improve once they know the tasks. The next check-ins form your baseline. After two baseline check-ins, each new result is compared with your own average and spread. A result is called lower than usual when it falls well outside that spread, by a margin larger than normal day-to-day noise.

Check-ins taken while sick, short on sleep, or interrupted are saved but left out of the baseline. You can change that on the Your data page.

What it can't do

Mindline can't diagnose dementia, mild cognitive impairment, or any other condition. It has no population norms, so it can't say how you compare with others your age. A lower score can come from stress, mood, hearing or vision, a new device, or simply a bad day. A steady score doesn't rule out a problem either.

If you or people close to you have noticed changes in memory, planning, or everyday tasks, talk to a doctor whatever your scores show. A clinician can do a proper assessment.

Privacy

Mindline has no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. Scores, answers and conversations are saved only in this browser on this device. You can delete everything at any time from the Your data page.

The voice assistant is the exception. When you talk with it, your words are sent to Anthropic's API to produce its replies, and on the results screen today's results are sent too. Voice input uses your browser's speech service, which in Chrome and Edge sends the audio to Google or Microsoft. With the assistant turned off, nothing leaves the device.

Prototype. Not a medical device and not reviewed by clinicians.
